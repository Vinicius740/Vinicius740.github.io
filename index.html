Exploratory Analysis of Sales Data from a Shoe Store Chain

Integrative Project (Projeto Integrador), Fatec Adamantina, 2026. Pair project.

Problem

A retail chain with three branches (Riyadh, Jeddah and Khobar) records thousands of sales, but raw transactions do not show which products, categories and months drive its results. Managers need clear indicators to make decisions.

What I Did

I carried out an exploratory analysis of the chain's sales data to evaluate commercial performance and find sales patterns, presenting the findings as indicators and charts that are easy to read.

How I Did It

I worked in Python on Google Colab with four datasets: sales (10,000 records), product catalog (365), branches (3) and sales targets (36).

  • Cleaning: checked for missing values and duplicates (none found in the sales data), converted dates and created revenue, year, month and day columns.
  • Standardization: trimmed spaces and lowercased the category and color fields.
  • Analysis: total revenue, units sold and average ticket, compared by branch, category, color, size, product and month.
  • Tools: Python, Pandas, Seaborn and Plotly.

Results

  • Boots had the highest revenue, followed by Heels and Formal. Sandals had the lowest.
  • The three branches performed very similarly: Khobar (about 1,680,000), Jeddah (about 1,675,000) and Riyadh (about 1,670,000).
  • In 2024, October had the highest revenue (about 459,500) and March the lowest (about 393,500).
  • The category with the most units sold is not the one with the highest revenue.
  • Size 41 sold the most units (about 6,020); size 36 sold the fewest (about 3,790).
  • The ten products with the highest revenue are all Boots. BO199 and BO934 lead with about 29,100 each.
